The biggest share of creator money now pays for ads

If your agency earns from paid media or production, or your team holds either budget, creator spending is now part of your plan whether you call it that or not. The largest share of what brands spend on creators pays to run creator content as ads, and much of the growth is coming out of budgets that already exist: TV, performance media and creative production.

Brands will spend $116bn on creators worldwide in 2026, up 15% from $101bn in 2025, according to Marketers to Spend $116 Billion on Creators in 2026, research from Madison and Wall published on 18 November. It forecasts $178bn by 2030. The study counts marketer spending only, so fan subscriptions, tips, merchandise and platform payouts are left out.

Where the money goes

Madison and Wall assigns every dollar to one of five segments that don't overlap. The largest, at 44% ($52bn), is paid media: paying to amplify creator content, including the ad space behind it. Second, at 28% ($33bn), is the familiar model of sponsored posts and brand ambassadors.

The third is smaller and growing fastest. At 12%, asset-led spending is brands paying creators for content rather than for their audience. It grew 20% a year from 2020 to 2025 and is forecast to grow 16% a year to 2030.

Then comes the line agencies will notice: agencies, talent managers and technology platforms keep 8% net between them.

For UK readers: Madison and Wall puts the UK at 5% of global creator spend in 2025, the largest market it breaks out after the US (43%). It says most markets are growing close to the 15% global rate, because multinational advertisers run similar plans across countries.

It comes out of budgets that already exist

Madison and Wall says creator growth is funded largely by moving existing budgets, rather than by new money. Its interviews across the industry point to TV, to performance media such as paid social, programmatic and search, and to creative production budgets.

We think that is the part of the report agencies should read twice, because for many agencies performance media and production are a large part of what they earn. The report also names where asset-led growth comes from: it is growing "as creators and AI tools win budget from creative agencies and production companies", in Madison and Wall's words.

Some agencies have built their own answer on the paid side. Admiral Media, a performance marketing agency for apps and games, describes in its Fastic case study the system it built for the fasting app. AI makes hundreds of versions of an ad, from fully AI-made concepts to ads made to look like customer videos. Each one is scored on live results while it runs, templates and brand rules keep everything looking like Fastic, and what works feeds the next round.

Admiral says the system cut Fastic's cost per result by 70%, and says the work won Silver in the AI category at The Drum Awards for Marketing EMEA 2026.

What to build

We think an agency whose creator offer is introductions is competing for part of that 8%, alongside talent managers and the platforms. In our view, the work that holds its value is deciding which creator and AI-made content gets paid media behind it, under which brand rules, and judged by which results.

That is a system, and someone has to build it: a way of testing, a rulebook the client signs off, and a definition of success agreed before the money goes out. It is also where AI is genuinely useful, because making many versions and keeping the ones that perform is the job Admiral describes.

For in-house teams, including small ones covering several markets, the consequence is practical. If creator content increasingly runs as ads, it needs the disciplines of any ad: what is being tested, what the brand allows, what counts as success and who owns the rights in each market. Because multinational advertisers run similar plans across markets, a team of three can set those rules once and reuse them everywhere, instead of briefing each creator from scratch.

A word of caution. Brands paying for content rather than reach is 12% of creator spend, so our reading is that, for now, this is a shift at the edge of the production line for many agencies, and restructuring in a panic would be a mistake. Equally, if creators and AI tools make content cheaper, defending old production rates is a losing argument. The case to make to clients is what comes alongside cheaper content: the choosing, the rules and the proof that it worked.

What to do this week

Agency leaders: ask finance for two numbers. The share of last year's production and paid social fees that came from work a creator or an AI tool could now do, and the clients who already buy creator content directly.

Pick the first part to build, and name an owner. Choosing which content gets paid media behind it, setting the brand rules, or deciding how results are judged. One of them, with one person accountable.

In-house leads: ask your agency to show its process. How it decides which creator content gets paid amplification, and how it reports the result. You want a method you can see, rather than a list of names.

Everyone: check the rights before you add media. Find out who owns the creator content you have already paid for, for how long and in which markets, before you put money behind it.

If you already run creator content through your paid plan, we would like to hear what you judge it on, and who in your team makes the call.
