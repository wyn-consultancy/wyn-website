Accenture bought a data firm. Agencies need to build their own edge

Accenture has bought Sagacity, a London-headquartered data and customer intelligence firm, to strengthen Accenture Song in the UK and Ireland. The deal was announced on 13 October in Accenture's newsroom, and the terms were not disclosed.

Sagacity was founded in 2005. Accenture says it brings "differentiated data assets", AI-enabled technology and more than 140 specialists, who join Accenture Song's UK and Ireland team and will work on everything from early strategy to running day-to-day operations. The release points to sectors with big customer bases and complicated customer journeys, naming utilities, retail, banking and telecoms as examples.

Sohel Aziz, senior managing director of Accenture Song UK and Ireland, gave the reasoning in one line: "Our clients are increasingly looking to connect data, AI and operations to drive growth and reinvent customer experiences."

What Accenture paid for

Read the release closely and the purchase is a bundle. Accenture gets Sagacity's data assets, the technology to work with them, and a team whose expertise is turning customer insight into action for clients. None of that comes with a software licence.

We think that is now the bar agencies compete against for customer work. A consultancy can walk into a utility or a bank with data assets, technology to use them and people who can act on both. An agency that walks into the same room saying it uses the same AI tools as everyone else is offering something the client could buy directly.

We see this as a clear case for one of our positions: agencies should build, not only buy. The edge comes from what you build.

Build the advantage one workflow at a time

Few agencies have the balance sheet for an acquisition. As we read it, the advantage Accenture paid for has two parts: distinctive data, and the ability to act on it. Both can be built at agency scale.

Agencies already hold more of the first than they give themselves credit for. Briefs, performance reports, research and customer feedback pass through the agency every month. In many teams, that material lives in decks and folders, and the same analysis gets rebuilt by hand each time it is needed.

In our experience, the second part is where most agencies fall short. Insight stays in a PDF because nobody has built the thing that turns it into a recommendation, on a schedule, connected to the client's own numbers. A small internal build can close that gap.

This connects to a second position we hold: integrate, don't install. An AI tool in a separate tab, fed by copy and paste, stays a toy. The same model wired into a client's data and your reporting becomes part of how the agency works, and something the client can't get from another supplier.

We have seen this first-hand inside agencies. Once the monthly client reports were automated, putting them together went from days of work to a few hours. The time that freed up went into the part clients pay for: analysing what the numbers meant and planning the next moves with accurate knowledge.

What it means for agencies and in-house teams

For agencies, this deal shows a consultancy packaging data, AI and delivery into one offer aimed at clients with large customer bases. If you pitch for customer work at a utility, retailer, bank or telecoms company, expect to meet that offer across the table.

The response that fits an agency's size starts with the client knowledge you already hold. Each workflow you build on it makes the next pitch easier to win on substance.

For in-house marketing teams, the useful question when comparing partners changes. Ask what an agency or consultancy has built itself and connected to data like yours, and ask to see it working on a problem you recognise.

What to do this week

  1. Pick one repeated analysis. From the client material your team handles each month, choose one analysis that gets rebuilt by hand every time.
  2. Build one small workflow around it this quarter. Aim for something that turns that data into a recommendation the client can act on. Keep it narrow, make it run reliably, and treat it as an agency asset you own.
  3. Rewrite your capabilities slide. Replace the list of AI tools with the things you have built and what each one does for the client.
  4. Ask your three largest clients which customer data they struggle to use. Their answers are your build roadmap, and they show you where the client already feels the gap that consultancies are now selling into.

All of this can start with one person who has protected time and a problem worth solving.
